From the plan to the final system

The Phase-1 deck listed fifteen things to build. This page goes through each one: what it became, why that version was chosen, and the measurement behind the choice. It ends with the explainability study, which explains most of the other decisions.

How each choice was made

Rule first, run second

Before each experiment, its pass or fail condition was written into the plan. When a result failed its rule, it was reported as a failure.

Fit on val, report on test

The 100 public Fishyscapes images are split 50/50. Every threshold, temperature and calibrator is fitted on the first half and scored on the second.

Every number has a log

Results are copied from the run logs, with paired bootstrap 95% intervals over images (20-frame blocks on video). Nothing on the site or in the thesis is typed in by hand.

The decision trail

The order matters here: each step was chosen because of what the previous one found.

Measure calibration where it matters

Found
Whole-image ECE is 0.0004, which looks excellent. Inside an 8-pixel band around object outlines it is 0.2273, and those edges are under-confident at every radius.
Why
Only 0.28% of pixels are anomalous, so the whole-image number is almost entirely easy background.
Chosen
Edge-band ECE at 4, 8 and 16 px as the calibration measure, reported next to whole-image ECE.

Get the baseline temperature right

Found
The first temperature, 1.71, had been fitted with the weighted training loss and never converged. Refitting with plain log-likelihood gives 1.2251.
Chosen
T = 1.2251, with every dependent number recomputed. The conclusions held, and the gap between image and edges grew.

Try every reasonable calibrator under the same rules

Found
The image wants T = 1.2251 and the edges want T = 4.3592; one temperature cannot serve both. A disagreement-based temperature is no better than a single one. The neighbourhood calibrator fixes edge ECE (0.0738) but drops AP to 0.4929, and its outline gain holds at only 2 of 6 thresholds. The calibration loss never beats the temperature at any weight.
Chosen
Report the analysis as the contribution, not a new calibration method. Every method failed at least one of its own rules.

Test outside the benchmark

Found
On the pasted CARLA route, edges are over-confident, and the calibration loss helps there. On RoadAnomaly21, TwinGuard falls below MSP.
Chosen
Keep both as results: one explains the calibration loss, the other marks the model's limit.

Ask why: explainability

Question
Why do pasted edges and real edges go in opposite directions?
Chosen
Five evaluation-only tests, each checked against a removal or a control before being trusted. Results below.

Explainability results

X1 · remove it
0.347 → 0.091

Inpainting a real object away cuts its score. Removing only its edges or interior raises the score instead, because the repaint itself gets flagged. This is a lower bound.

X1b · remove it exactly
0.951 → 0.016

On the CARLA route the clean frame is a perfect removal. Inpainting the same spot leaves 0.625, so the fill adds +0.609 [+0.538, +0.681] on its own.

X5 · same photo
0.94 vs 0.35

Pasted objects in real photos score near 1 with over-confident edges; real objects in the same photos score 0.35 with under-confident edges. Edge gap +0.468 [+0.401, +0.532]. Objects seen in training score the same as unseen ones (−0.011): the model reacts to pasting, not to the object.

X2 · which stage
25% agreement

On real objects the gradient map matches the removal test only at chance. It points at the coarsest stage, but removing evidence shows real detections rely on the middle stages. On pastes the agreement is 56% and passes.

X3 · kind of uncertainty
0.236 vs 0.010

At object edges almost all uncertainty is shared ambiguity, not disagreement between heads. Disagreement shows where edges are, not which side is wrong.

What it adds up to The model has learned what a pasted object looks like. That is why calibration learned on pastes moves real edges the wrong way, why more pasted CARLA objects would not help (item 4), and why the next real improvement has to come from more realistic training anomalies, not from tuning the calibrator.

Still open

Two checks remain before the thesis numbers are final: a visual review of the CARLA mask audit, which fixes the exact count of misaligned objects (currently 15 to 21 of 45), and confirming that the local COCO bank is the copy used in training, which turns the "no memorisation" result from provisional to firm.
